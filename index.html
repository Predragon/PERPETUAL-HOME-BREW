Why brew your own

A sports drink with live cultures

Store sports drinks replace the salt you lose in sweat, mostly with sugar and flavoring. This tonic replaces the same salt, adds live lactic-acid bacteria and costs about as much as a cup of tea.

More sodium, less sugar

Sweat is mostly salt and water. The tonic has about 600 mg sodium per liter, more than most store drinks. The culture eats most of the 12 g of sugar per liter, so what's left is tart, not syrupy.

Live cultures

The sauerkraut brine brings lactic-acid bacteria, the same family found in yogurt and kefir. In a 2021 Stanford study, people who ate more fermented foods had a more diverse gut microbiome and lower inflammation markers.

You control every ingredient

Nothing goes in except tea, sugar, salt, vinegar and fruit. No dyes or sweeteners. Change the salt for hot days or the caffeine for evening sessions.

Cheap, and it uses scraps

A liter costs about 20–30 cents in tea bags, sugar, salt and vinegar. The flavor comes from peels you'd otherwise throw away.

General health

About even

Neither is proven. Claims that kombucha "detoxes" your body have no evidence behind them.

Probiotics

Kefir beats both

Grain kefir carries far more strains, in much higher numbers, and has the most human research.

The Stanford result came from eating a variety of fermented foods, not any single one. Drinking the tonic around workouts, having kefir daily and eating the sauerkraut covers more than switching to one drink. If you enjoy kombucha, keep it as a treat.

Tonic wins
  • More sodium and about 80% less sugar than regular Gatorade or Powerade
  • Gentler on teeth. Most sports drinks use citric acid at pH ~3.
  • Live cultures, no dyes or sweeteners, and a fraction of the cost

Equipment

What you need

Brewing

  • 5 L protein-powder jar, washed well. Replace it once the inside is scratched or cloudy.
  • Fine strainer and a funnel
  • Long spoon or tongs used only for the brew
  • Small pot or kettle for the tea concentrate

Bottling

  • Plastic soda bottles, 500 mL–1 L. Bottles made for fizzy drinks handle pressure better than thin water bottles.
  • A small jar of red-cabbage test liquid (see pH test)
Day 0 · once

Starter batch (4 liters)

3.5 Lwater
7 bagsblack tea
4 tbspwhite sugar (about 50 g)
500 mLlive cabbage brine, from homemade sauerkraut or refrigerated unpasteurized sauerkraut. Shelf-stable jars are pasteurized, so the bacteria in them are dead.
4 tbspsugarcane vinegar, 4–5% acidity (¼ cup)
1 setpeels from a flavor combo
No sauerkraut brine? Buy refrigerated unpasteurized sauerkraut, or make your own. The quick cabbage juice is ready in 3–5 days.

No extra salt in the starter. The brine provides about 1 g of sodium per liter at the start, and this settles to about 600 mg once the daily feeds take over.

  1. Make a concentrate. Steep the tea bags in 1 L of boiling water for 5–10 minutes. Remove the bags and stir in the sugar until it dissolves.
  2. Cool it. Add 2.5 L of cold water. Check the temperature with a clean finger. It should feel cool or barely lukewarm, below 30 °C, because heat kills the culture.
  3. Start the culture. Pour everything into the jar: tea, brine, vinegar and peels.
  4. Loose lid. Screw the lid on, then back it off a quarter turn so gas can escape. Keep it out of the sun, ideally at 20–26 °C.
  5. Wait 2–4 days. It's ready when it tastes clearly tart, about like lemonade, and turns the test liquid pink. Small bubbles and cloudiness are normal. Then start the daily cycle.
Every day

The daily cycle

  1. Taste and test. The brew should taste tart and turn the test liquid pink. If it's purple, skip today's harvest and see Troubleshooting.
  2. Harvest 1 L. Pour it through the strainer with clean hands and a clean funnel.
  3. Bottle it. Leave about 6 cm (2.5 in) of headspace. Add bottle add-ins if you like. For more fizz, add ½ tsp sugar per 500 mL.
  4. Feed the jar right away using the amounts below.
  5. Squeeze and chill. Cap the bottles tight and leave them on the counter. Squeeze them a few times a day. Move them to the fridge when they feel firm with only slight give, usually after 12–36 hours. Drink within a week and open slowly over the sink.

Daily feed

Steep the tea bags in the hot water for 5 minutes, then stir in the sugar and salt. Remove the bags and add the cold water. The mix is cool enough to pour in right away. Use 2 tbsp of vinegar per liter if the brew tasted flat or the test looked purple.

Every 4–7 days

Swap the peels

Remove soft or pale peels with the clean spoon and add fresh ones. Take pineapple skins out after 3 days because they ferment strongly.

When it slows

Add more brine

If bottles take more than 48 hours to firm up, stir in 100–150 mL of fresh cabbage brine.

Monthly

Reset the jar

Save 1 L of brew. Wash the jar with hot soapy water and rinse it well. Restart with the saved liter plus 3 L of fresh sweet tea, 2 tbsp vinegar, ½ tsp salt and fresh peels.

Going away

Pause it

Feed it, tighten the lid and put the jar in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. Let it warm up for a day before your next harvest.

Your question

Should I add brewer's yeast?

Not as a regular step. The fruit skins already carry wild yeast. Here's what each kind of yeast does:

Won't ferment

Brewer's / nutritional yeast flakes

The yeast is dead. It adds B vitamins and a savory yeasty taste but no fizz. If you want the nutrition, stir it into food.

Avoid in the jar

Active dry, baker's or beer yeast

It crowds out the wild culture. The brew turns beery and less sour, makes more alcohol, and bottles can turn hard in a few hours.

Better boost

Fresh skins, brine, warmth

Apple, grape and pineapple skins are full of wild yeast. Add fresh brine and move the jar somewhere at 24–26 °C.

Only use it as a rescue. If bottles won't firm up after 3 days somewhere warm, add 3–4 grains of dry yeast to one bottle and check it every 2–3 hours.
About alcohol: wild fermentation always makes a little. Fresh harvests usually stay under about 1%, but bottles left warm for a long time get stronger.
Kefir pairings

Kefir sports drinks

Salted fermented-milk drinks like ayran and doogh are traditional thirst quenchers. Kefir plus your tonic makes a tangy, fizzy recovery drink with protein.

Two rules: Keep kefir and kefir grains out of the jar. Milk grains need milk, and dairy spoils a perpetual brew. Mix in the glass and drink the same day. Never seal kefir mixes in a bottle to build fizz.

Protect your teeth

The tonic is acidic, like every sports drink. Sipping it through a long workout keeps your teeth bathed in acid, so:

  • Rinse or swish with plain water after drinking.
  • Wait 30–60 minutes before brushing. Enamel is soft right after acid.
  • Use a straw or a sports cap so less of it washes over your teeth.
  • Drink in a few bigger gulps rather than constant small sips.
  • The kefir drinks are gentle on teeth, because dairy buffers the acid.
Check with a doctor first if you're pregnant, immunocompromised, on a low-sodium diet, have kidney disease, or take blood-pressure medication.
No strips needed

Red-cabbage pH test

Red cabbage juice changes color with acidity. Low pH is what keeps a wild ferment safe.

  1. Make the indicator. Chop 2–3 red cabbage leaves and pour a cup of boiling water over them. After 10 minutes, strain the purple liquid into a jar. It keeps about a week in the fridge.
  2. Set up references. In three white cups, add 1 tbsp water, 1 tbsp vinegar and 1 tbsp of your brew.
  3. Add 1 tbsp of indicator to each cup and compare. Your brew should look close to the vinegar cup and clearly different from the water cup.

Troubleshooting & safety

Mold or yeast? What to look for

Normal

Brown strings or sediment on the bottom

This is yeast. It's harmless. Leave it or strain it out.

Normal

Smooth, clear jelly disc on top

This is cellulose from vinegar bacteria, a baby SCOBY. It's harmless. Remove it or leave it.

Fix

Flat white or cream film, wrinkled or powdery

This is kahm yeast, which is harmless but tastes stale. Skim it off, add 1 tbsp vinegar per liter and harvest more often.

Fix

Tastes like sweet tea after 3 days, or the test looks purple

Stir in 2 tbsp vinegar per liter and 150 mL brine. Don't drink it until the test turns pink.

Fix

Nail-polish or solvent smell

The yeast is stressed from heat or waiting too long between feeds. Harvest, feed and move the jar somewhere cooler.

Fix

Thick, slimy or stringy liquid

Some lactic bacteria do this and it usually clears in a few days. Throw it out if it also smells bad.

Fix

Bottles won't firm up in 48 hours

Move them somewhere warmer, add ½ tsp sugar per 500 mL, use fresh skins in the jar and add more brine.

Fix

Bottles gush when opened

Chill them earlier, at firm instead of hard, and keep them cold for 24 hours before opening. Open slowly over the sink.

Fix

Too sour or too salty

If too sour, harvest more often and cut the vinegar in the feed to 1 tsp per liter. If too salty, leave the salt out of the next 2 feeds.

Discard

Fuzzy, raised or dry spots, or blue, green, black, pink or orange patches

This is mold. Throw out everything, including the peels. Wash the jar with hot soapy water and restart. Skimming mold off doesn't make the rest safe.

Discard

Rotten, putrid or garbage smell, or a blue or green test

Throw it out and restart with fresh brine.

House rules

  • If you're unsure whether it's safe, throw it out. A new batch costs a few tea bags.
  • Wash your hands and use clean utensils. Never dip a used cup into the jar.
  • Use plastic, glass or stainless steel. Acid eats aluminum and copper.
  • Good smells are tart, fruity, bready and slightly like sauerkraut.
Extra · make your own starter

Fermented cabbage

The brine from homemade sauerkraut starts the tonic and recharges it later. You need 500 mL to start and about 150 mL now and then. Choose the quick juice if you want brine in a few days, or full sauerkraut for brine and a side dish.

3–5 days · all liquid

Quick cabbage juice

3 cupschopped green cabbage (about 300 g)
1 Lwater
1½ tspsalt (about 9 g)
  1. Pulse everything in a blender for a few seconds until the cabbage is in small bits, not a smooth purée.
  2. Pour it into a clean jar and leave 3 cm of space at the top. Put the lid on loosely.
  3. Leave it at room temperature for 3–5 days and stir once a day.
  4. It's ready when it smells sour and pickle-like and turns the test liquid pink. Strain it, then keep the juice in the fridge for up to a month.
1–4 weeks · brine + sauerkraut

Classic sauerkraut

1 kggreen cabbage (1 medium head)
20 gsalt, 2% of the cabbage weight (about 1 heaped tbsp)
500 mLextra 2% brine: 10 g salt dissolved in 500 mL water. This gives you more liquid for the tonic.
  1. Take off 2 clean outer leaves and set them aside. Quarter the cabbage, cut out the core and slice it thin.
  2. Sprinkle on the salt and squeeze and massage it with your hands for 5–10 minutes, until it's limp and sitting in its own juice.
  3. Pack it tightly into a jar and press it down hard. Pour in the juice from the bowl and the extra brine.
  4. Cover the top with the saved leaves and weigh them down with a small water-filled jar or a zip bag of brine. Everything must stay under the liquid.
  5. Put the lid on loosely and keep the jar at 18–22 °C out of the sun. Press it down every day for the first few days.
  6. Taste it after 1 week. When it's pleasantly sour, pour off the brine for the tonic and move the sauerkraut to the fridge to eat.
Tips. Use plain salt without iodine if you can find it, since iodized salt can make the brine cloudy. Weigh the salt rather than measuring by spoon: too little lets spoilage in, too much stalls the ferment. Red cabbage works too, but it turns the tonic pink and makes the pH test hard to read. Save 150 mL of old brine to start the next jar faster.
Kraut safety: anything above the brine can spoil. If fuzzy or colored mold appears, throw out the jar. A flat white film is kahm yeast: skim it off and push the cabbage back under the brine.
